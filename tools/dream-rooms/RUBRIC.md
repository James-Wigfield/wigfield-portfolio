# Dream Rooms · the review rubric

Read the frames (`dream-rooms-work/<id>/frames/`) next to `raw.txt`, at most **two rounds**. Score each question 0 (fails), 1 (so-so) or 2 (yes). Then fix the **three worst** problems in `world.json` or the bespoke scripts, and write what's still off in `reading.md` (in your own words about the build, never his dream words copied into a log).

| # | Question | Fails when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Is the light sourceless?** | You can point at the lamp. Light pools under fixtures the dream never named. It's evenly lit like a render. |
| 2 | **Is anything tidy, generic or game-like?** | Clean edges everywhere, a symmetric room, props in a neat row, a "level" you'd recognise, a texture that's obviously a stock tile. |
| 3 | **Are his details there, and crisp?** | A thing he named is missing, too small, blurred into the fog, or replaced by a generic stand-in. Named things must read first. |
| 4 | **Is the scale off?** | Everything is believable. Something should be 10–25 % wrong: a hall too big, doors too tall, a corridor too narrow. |
| 5 | **Is it empty?** | Furniture or detail he didn't mention fills the space. Empty and too quiet is the point. |
| 6 | **Is there a Backrooms default he didn't ask for?** | Drop ceilings, fluorescent panels, yellow striped wallpaper or olive fog, without a `for` that cites his words. |
| 7 | **Is anything invented that the route doesn't need?** | An `invented: true` thing that isn't connective tissue (a joining passage, a floor, a wall, a doorway the route needs), or an invented thing with detail or colour. |
| 8 | **Do the strange parts happen?** | A teleport you can see coming, a change that animates on screen, a dissolve that reads as a loading screen, a figure that looks like a person. |
| 9 | **Does it end by waking?** | It ends on a wall or a menu. The wake line isn't his words. |

A frame set passes when nothing scores 0 and the total is at least 13 of 18 (questions 1–9 × 2).

For the corridor (`/dreams`), ask instead: *does this feel like a dreamy corridor of doors, a game menu, or the Backrooms, and why?*
