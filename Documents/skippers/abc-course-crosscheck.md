# ABC online course vs `/skippers`: cross-check (2026-11-22)

ABC's WA RST online course (boatsafetycourse.abcboating.com/courses/wa-rst, logged in as James) has:
- 5 lessons (Trip Planning, Navigation, Weather & Tides, Emergencies, PWC) and 59 topics, mostly video.
- 5 lesson quizzes, 157 questions in all.
- A 154-question final quiz. It needs 100% to pass, and it comes with no hints and no answer key in the page.

I read every quiz question and checked each one against the 2026 workbook and the site's 359-question bank, cards and facts. ABC's wording is not copied anywhere.

## Headline

- **Every ABC quiz topic is already covered by `/skippers`, except for the gaps listed below.** That includes:
  - 8 knots near jetties, swimmers, moored boats, the bank, water under 3 m and within 15 m of a vessel underway
  - jetty right of way
  - skiing ages and distances
  - lights, marks, leads, flags and sound signals
  - the gear table, EPIRBs, radio and Mayday / Pan Pan
  - flotation, anchoring, maintenance, refuelling, chart datum, PWC freestyle distances, alcohol, registration and pollution
- **ABC's quiz is updated to the 2026 rules (400 m and 4 NM).** This corrects run-notes item 3, which feared the quiz used the old 2/5 NM distances. One leftover question says "3 nautical miles": under 2026 rules that's still inside 4 NM, so no radio is needed. The site agrees.
- **Protected waters:** ABC counts "within 400 m of the mainland" as protected water. That matches workbook p.49 and the site, which resolves run-notes open question 1(a) in practice.
- **27 MHz:** ABC still teaches 27.88 MHz alongside VHF 16, and so does the site (27 MHz ch 88). VHF needs an operator's certificate; 27 MHz doesn't.

## Gaps found and fixed (6 questions + 3 card lines)

| Gap (ABC asks it, site didn't teach it) | Workbook | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Crab nets, cray pots and floats not allowed in a channel, fairway or passage; never tie up to a beacon | p.12 | `rules-066`, `rules-067`, plus a line on the "Anchoring don'ts" card |
| Extinguisher colour bands: white = dry powder, black = CO₂, blue = foam, yellow = vaporising liquid | p.65 (chart) | `safety-080`, `safety-081`, plus a line on the "Fire extinguishers" card |
| Cold front: squalls and rain, then showers, and the wind shifts | p.79 | `operations-039`, plus a line on the "Bad weather coming" card |
| Wave height depends on wind speed, duration and fetch | p.78 | `operations-038`, plus the same card |

## ABC asks it, but the workbook doesn't teach it (know these for the quiz)

- **Wind against tide makes the roughest sea.** This is general seamanship and isn't stated in the workbook.
- **A red parachute flare is the long-range flare.** ABC asks which flare to use at about 9 km from the mainland by day. The workbook lists red parachute flares only as a recognised distress signal (p.84) and gives no range or height, so the site makes no claim. For ABC's question, the long-range answer is the parachute flare.
- **The PWC "60 km/h covers 100 m in 6 s" question** is about reaction time, and answering it is just reasoning.
- **Some questions depend on pictures** (cardinal topmarks, night lights, flags). The site's labs drill all of them.

## How to use it

- The online course is optional. Use ABC's final quiz as the last check: aim for 100%, and look up any miss in the site's Exam room.
